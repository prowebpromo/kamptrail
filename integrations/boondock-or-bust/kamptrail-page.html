Find free and paid campsites, see public land, and plan your stops with KampTrail. It is free to use and requires no account.

Search by city, state, campsite name, or coordinates. Each listing shows its source and, where the source publishes it, the exact fee wording.

How to find free camping

  1. Open Filters and choose Free. These are listings whose source says there is no camping fee. Free with conditions means camping is free but something else applies, such as a pass, a season, or an extra-vehicle charge.
  2. Open a listing to read the source's own fee wording and the date it was imported. A free camping fee does not cover entrance fees, permits, or reservation charges.
  3. Open the layers button and turn on Public land. Long-press any spot to see who manages it and that agency's stay-limit rule. BLM says most of its land allows dispersed camping unless an area is posted closed.

Plan a trip

  1. Choose Save stop on a campsite, or long-press the map to save any spot.
  2. Open Saved stops to reorder stops, add notes and dates, and get drive times.
  3. Choose Directions for the whole trip to open it in Google Maps, or export a GPX file for another app.

RV length, hookups, and services

Enter your rig length in Filters to see campgrounds where at least one site lists that length. Hookup filters work the same way. Both use Recreation.gov site records, so most free and dispersed sites have no length on file. You can include those listings, clearly labeled. Turn on RV services to see dump stations and propane from OpenStreetMap contributors. Call ahead, because hours are not verified.

What the map can tell you

KampTrail combines campsite records from Recreation.gov, the US Forest Service, the Bureau of Land Management, and OpenStreetMap contributors. Coverage is incomplete and varies by state. The app’s About the data panel shows the current catalogue count and sources.

Listings have not been field verified. Confirm current fees, camping permission, seasonal closures, reservations, and suitability for your vehicle with the land manager or operator. A location on public land does not by itself establish permission to camp.

Using KampTrail without signal

Open Offline on the map, zoom to the area you will camp in, and choose Save this area. KampTrail saves the USGS Topo map for that area. When you lose signal, choose Topo in the layers menu. Listings you have viewed, saved stops, and dump and propane locations also stay available. Forecasts, land manager lookups, drive times, and directions need internet. KampTrail does not provide turn-by-turn navigation or cell coverage maps.

Saved stops and offline maps belong to this browser on this device. Clearing browser data removes them. Export a GPX file to back up your stops. Embedded and full-page versions may keep separate saved stops depending on browser privacy settings. For regular use, open the full-page version.
